1 Background

Here we are looking at a multifactorial differential gene expression analysis for developing coral embryos at 3 developmental stages (4 hours post fertilization , 9 hours post fertilization, and 14 hours post fertiliation). Embryos at each stage were exposed to either a control, low, mid, or high concentration of PVC leachate. This work aims to understand sublethal effects of microplastic pollution on coral reefs.

1.1 Inputs

  • gcm_filt.csv is the unfiltered gene count matrix for 61 samples (two outlier samples were already removed).
  • metadata_filt.csv is the metadata for 61 samples

1.2 Outputs

  • volcano_facet.png

VST compresses the high-end of the count distribution and expands the low-end, making the variance roughly constant across the range. The resulting values are on an approximate log2 scale but not exact log2(count + 1) — VST is more nuanced, especially for low counts. A range of 5 to 15 corresponds roughly to raw counts ranging from 2³² to 2¹⁵ ≈ 32 to 32,000, so it’s realistic for RNA-seq gene expression data.

5 Run DESeq Test

This calculates the log2fold change between factors

dds <- DESeq(dds, test = "LRT", reduced = ~ hpf + leachate)

This compares:

Full model: ~ hpf + leachate + hpf:leachate

Reduced model: ~ hpf + leachate

So it asks: Does including the interaction explain significantly more variation? If yes, then some genes respond to leachate differently at different hpf stages.

7 Effect of high exposure at 14 hpf

When you test for main effects of leachate (e.g., “leachate_high_vs_control”), it’s at the reference timepoint (hpf = 4). The interaction terms model how the leachate effect changes relative to that baseline as development progresses.

What’s the effect of high exposure at 14 hpf? You must combine: - “leachate_high_vs_control”: main leachate effect at 4 hpf - “hpf14.leachatehigh”: additional effect at 14 hpf

9.1 Cooks distance

DESeq2 relies on the negative binomial distribution to make estimates and perform statistical inference on differences. While the negative binomial is versatile in having a mean and dispersion parameter, extreme counts in individual samples might not fit well to the negative binomial. For this reason, we perform automatic detection of count outliers. We use Cook’s distance, which is a measure of how much the fitted coefficients would change if an individual sample were removed (Cook 1977). For more on the implementation of Cook’s distance see the manual page for the results function. Below we plot the maximum value of Cook’s distance for each row over the rank of the test statistic to justify its use as a filtering criterion.

dds <- DESeq(dds, test = "LRT", reduced = ~ hpf + leachate)

This compares:

Full model: \~ hpf + leachate + hpf:leachate

Reduced model: \~ hpf + leachate

So it asks: Does including the interaction explain significantly more variation? If yes, then some genes respond to leachate differently at different hpf stages.

## Cooks distance

> DESeq2 relies on the negative binomial distribution to make estimates and perform statistical inference on differences. While the negative binomial is versatile in having a mean and dispersion parameter, extreme counts in individual samples might not fit well to the negative binomial. For this reason, we perform automatic detection of count outliers. We use Cook's distance, which is a measure of how much the fitted coefficients would change if an individual sample were removed (Cook 1977). For more on the implementation of Cook's distance see the manual page for the *results* function.
